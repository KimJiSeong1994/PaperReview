          Standard factuality evaluations of LLMs treat all errors alike, obscuring whether failures arise from
          missing knowledge (empty shelves) or from limited access to encoded facts (lost keys). We propose
         a behavioral framework that profiles factual knowledge at the level of facts rather than questions,
           characterizing each fact by whether it is encoded, and then by how accessible it is: cannot be recalled,
          can be directly recalled, or can only be recalled with inference-time computation (thinking). To support
          such profiling, we introduce WikiProfile, a new benchmark constructed via an automated pipeline with a
         prompted LLM grounded in web search. Across 4 million responses from 13 LLMs, we find that encoding2026              is nearly saturated in frontier models on our benchmark, with GPT-5 and Gemini-3 encoding 95–98%

D.4. Multiple Response Generation

For each task, we generate 𝑛=8 responses per question
for three reasons. First, our goal is to assess factual
knowledge at the level of facts; a single response is a
noisy estimator and may misclassify a fact as not en-
coded or not known due to sampling variance. Second,
because our questions are not single-token predictions,
probability-based estimates are not well defined, and
token-level likelihoods are inaccessible for most pro-
prietary LLMs, making repeated sampling a natural
alternative. Finally, multiple responses are required to
distinguish availability (at least one correct response)
from robustness (all responses correct), which is part
of our analysis of inference-time thinking.

  To assess the effect of multiple sampling and justify
our choice of 𝑛=8, we analyze how the fraction of facts
with 𝑔(𝑞) > 0.5 varies with 𝑛across four tasks: two
encoding tasks (completion and contextual) and two
knowledge tasks (direct and reverse). For each task
and model, we perform 1,000 bootstrap resamples,
each time subsampling 𝑛responses per question and
recomputing the fraction of facts with 𝑔(𝑞) >0.5. Fig-
ure 19 shows the width of the 90% empirical bootstrap
confidence interval (95th–5th percentiles) as a func-

* The left -context clearly constrains the **type** of entity expected next (e.g., a food , a

* The left -context is too vague or underspecified to narrow the completion to a single ,

     the entity).

* Omit non -essential modifiers (e.g., "Pulitzer Prize for Fiction" vs. "2003 Pulitzer Prize
